Simplifying Account Opening

The Upstox account opening flow was 24 screens. We got it to 16 without dropping a single compliance check. This is how.

Challenge

A 24-step KYC flow was losing roughly a quarter of starters before their first trade, with the biggest cliffs at the identity steps.

Move

Cut only the steps we'd added on top of the regulator's, fold three identity asks into one, and rebuild the ending so it feels like the app you signed up for.

Result

24 steps → 16, three identity touchpoints → one, with every mandated compliance capture intact.

Industry
Fintech / Investing
Role
Product Designer II
Timeline
10 Weeks
Company
Upstox
Team

Overview

Account opening is the first thing every Upstox user does. If they can't get through it, nothing else matters. A confusing screen at step seven doesn't just cost you step seven; it costs you everyone who would have finished eight through twenty-four.

I spent about ten weeks in this flow. Audited every screen against ten competitors. Read four months of drop-off data. Then rebuilt it around two ideas: fewer steps, and asking one clear thing at a time.

Problem

Five things kept showing up in the data:

  • Twenty-four steps, with branches. Nobody knew how much longer they had to go.
  • Two big cliffs in the drop-off chart. PAN and DOB were losing 22% of starters. Personal Details lost another 18%.
  • We were asking for identity four times. PAN, then DOB, then Digilocker, then Aadhaar. Overlapping data, four ways to fail.
  • Micro-screens made decisions bigger than they needed to be. Picking a segment, picking a plan, picking a payment mode. Each one its own screen, each one a chance to bail.
  • The screen at the end of the flow read like a receipt. No warmth, no product, no Upstox.

Why This Problem Mattered

Roughly a quarter of the people who started a session never got to a first trade. That's the cost of the current flow. Every step we don't need multiplies across millions of new accounts a year.

New flow vs the 24-step one we started with.

Industry Context

Every broker in India runs the same regulated identity gauntlet. The only question is how heavy they make it feel.

What we looked at

Ten apps: Groww, Sahi PRO, Zerodha, Dhan, Angel One, 5 Paisa, Fyers, Paytm Money, Share Market, HDFC Sky, plus ours. Two axes: how heavy does each screen feel, and how many screens does the whole thing take. We walked each flow end to end and captured every screen.

What we saw

The trade-off kept repeating. Apps that felt light took too many clicks. Apps with few clicks felt heavy. Sahi PRO was lovely but didn't read like a trading app. Zerodha was clean but wordy. Dhan was compact but cluttered and had no welcome at the end. HDFC Sky felt fine per screen but the count kept climbing. Nobody had gotten both axes right.

“The gap wasn't inventing something new. It was being the first flow that felt calm and finished fast.”

Audit note

Research & Discovery

Four months of screen-by-screen drop-off data. Three places where most of the damage was happening.

Zone 01 · 22% drop
Identity verification
What we saw

PAN and DOB was losing 22% of starters. People hesitated because they didn't remember their PAN. Then Digilocker asked for the Aadhaar number and a six-digit PIN, both from memory. Second bounce, right there.

What was really going on

We were asking for identity three times, when we only needed to check it once. Three chances to fail one thing.

Zone 02 · 18% drop
Personal details form
What we saw

Personal Details-1 was losing 18%. Too many fields at once. Trading experience, occupation, income, none of it obviously connected to “I'm trying to open an account”.

What was really going on

We were asking for personal info before we'd earned it. People give it up later, once they can see where the flow is going. Not on screen four.

Zone 03 · Decision fatigue
Payment and plan
What we saw

A standalone plan screen. A separate payment-mode picker. A page of price and T&C. Three interruptions right when someone was almost done.

What was really going on

Three small screens doing the job of one big one. Each one added a fresh doubt at the point of least tolerance.

Competitive Landscape

Three positions on the “light vs short” grid, and what each one taught us.

Light, but long
Groww
What they got right

Small priming screens tell you what's coming. Nice post-AO experience. Auto-creates a UPI ID so people can skip the penny drop.

What they didn't

Feels light per screen, but the count adds up. By the end you've tapped a lot.

Light and short
Sahi PRO
What they got right

Genuinely delightful. Progress is visible at every step. Custom in-flow screens (like a UPI app picker) remove entire moments of friction.

What they didn't

Doesn't look like a trading app. And there's no lead mode while the account is opening, so you're stuck watching a spinner.

Heavy but short
Dhan
What they got right

Fewer clicks. Multiple sub-steps live on one screen. No long priming.

What they didn't

Pages feel stuffed. PAN and Digilocker are asked for twice. Layouts nest and expand. There's no welcome after everything's done.

“The apps that felt light were long. The apps that were short felt heavy. We wanted both.”

Synthesis note

Problem Definition

Three jobs every step of the flow had to do, at the same time.

Functional
Let me finish without getting lost

Show me where I am, how much is left, and what the next screen wants from me before it asks.

Emotional
Make it feel like the app I signed up for

From screen one, I should feel like I'm inside Upstox. Not a KYC form that Upstox happens to sit behind.

Behavioural
Give me a reason to trust each ask

Before I type my PAN, my bank details, or draw my signature, tell me why you need it and where it goes.

Design Challenges

Three places where the project kept getting hard.

01
Cut the flow without cutting compliance

SEBI, NSDL, KRA, and the depository each require specific fields. Those weren't going anywhere. The steps we could cut were the ones we'd added on top: redundant identity asks, decision-fatigue mid-screens, and explainer pages that could just be inline text.

02
Heavy data, light room

This flow collects PAN, Aadhaar, bank data, photo, signature, nominee, and income info in one sitting. Any of those, on a bad screen, is a bailout. Every screen got one ask, one visual, and one clear signal of how much was left.

03
Make it feel like Upstox all the way through

A user in research told us the last screen “felt impersonal and clinical”. She'd just spent 20 minutes opening an account and the app said thanks like a receipt. The whole redesign treats onboarding as part of the app, not a form that sits in front of it. Same voice, same type, same motion.

Design Solutions

Six decisions that did most of the work.

Constraints we designed inside

SEBI, NSDL, KRA and the depository each mandate specific captures. Those steps were fixed and non-negotiable. The Digilocker and account-aggregator handshakes were third-party flows we couldn't restyle. So the only steps on the table were the ones we'd added on top: redundant asks, decision-fatigue mid-screens, and explainer pages that could become inline copy.

01
Identity

One identity moment instead of three

The NSDL PAN check runs quietly in the background. Users go straight into Digilocker. PAN and DOB don't need their own screen anymore. If the check fails, we ask inline. Three screens down to one.

Decision

Run the PAN check in the background and send people straight to Digilocker, collapsing PAN + DOB + Digilocker into one identity step.

Why

The drop-off data put the single biggest cliff (22%) at the PAN/DOB screen. The root cause was repetition, not any one field. We were asking for identity three separate times. Checking once and reusing it removes two of the three chances to fail.

Rejected

Pre-filling everything from PAN alone. It couldn't satisfy the KRA address and photo requirements, so Digilocker still had to happen.

02
Removed

Kill the standalone plan screen

Pricing moves into the welcome video and inline help on the registration screen. T&C gets its own moment at consent, where it belongs. We removed the screen without losing a word of disclosure.

03
Form ergonomics

A signature that fits, a selfie that works first try

Rotating the signature box to landscape gives you actual room to sign. The selfie step now has a live face mask and framing hints, so the photo passes the first time instead of the third.

04
Trust

UPI first, bank statement as backup

UPI app selection sits on the same screen as payment mode. One tap, one OTP, done. The slower account-aggregator path is there when UPI can't handle it. Above the field, a short line about how the data is used, in plain English.

05
Compliance, gently

Nominee, moved up, explained in ten seconds

Nominee used to sit at the end and get skipped constantly. It moved earlier and got a ten-second explainer about the SEBI mandate. More people complete it, fewer skip it.

06
Welcome

An ending that feels like the beginning

The old closure screen was a receipt. The new one greets you by name, points you at the first thing to try, and puts a real face on it (an influencer or product person, depending on segment). It doesn't stop feeling like Upstox at the finish line.

Design Iterations

Two 16-step routes carried into research. Same length, very different feel.

V0
Where we started

Twenty-four steps. Branched paths. Five bad drop-off zones. Visually heavy without being clear. This was the flow we were trying to fix.

V1
Explain first, ask second

Sixteen steps. Every screen opens with a small explainer: what we're about to ask, why, and where the data goes. The idea was to prevent the mistake before it happens.

V2
Ask first, explain if asked

Sixteen steps. Explainers hidden behind a tap. Confident users move fast; first-timers can pull the context in when they need it.

V3
What we shipped

V1 on identity and bank steps, where the stakes are highest. V2 on personal details and nominee, where confident users want to move. Best of each, tested against the original drop-off zones.

Usability Testing

Moderated sessions with first-time investors and lapsed users (people who had started the flow on a competitor and bailed).

What we wanted to find out

  • Did folding PAN and Digilocker into one step actually stop the biggest drop-off?
  • Did the horizontal signature feel like an obvious improvement, or just a different weird?
  • Did having a visible progress bar make the long sitting feel shorter?
  • Did the new closure screen change how “done” the whole thing felt at the end?

Key Learnings

What this project changed my mind about.

Onboarding isn't the place to teach the product

Onboarding is where you help someone finish. Product tours land after the first trade, not before the first field. People will sit through education once they've invested in something.

Identity checks shouldn't feel like a security queue

Asking for PAN, DOB, Aadhaar, and a PIN back to back is exhausting even when you have nothing to hide. Fetch it once, in the background, and surface the field only if the check fails.

Brand voice has to live in onboarding too

If the app feels warm and the onboarding reads like a form, people notice the seam. Rewriting the closure screen did more for retention than several flow-step cuts.

People sit through a long flow if they can see the end

Hide the progress bar and a short flow feels endless. Show it clearly and a long one becomes tolerable. Progress design is conversion design.

Future Opportunities

Where the flow should go next.

AA
Bring the account aggregator in-house

Right now the AA flow redirects to OneMoney's UI, which looks nothing like our app. When the AA APIs are ready, the whole thing lives inside Upstox instead of feeling like a stopover.

PWA
A welcome that knows what you signed up for

Someone opening for equities and someone opening for F&O don't want the same first screen. Same flow, different copy at the end.

RE
Get back the people who dropped off

Mid-flow drop-offs are the best re-engagement opportunity Upstox has. Push, email, and a one-tap resume link could win back a real chunk of that lost 27%.

F&O
Turn F&O activation on inside the flow

F&O activation lives after AO and gets skipped. Give serious traders an opt-in moment inside the flow so they don't need a second journey.

FTU
One first-time experience, end to end

The closing screen and the in-app FTUE are owned by different teams. Designing them as one thing, from signup to first trade, is the biggest unlock still on the table.
