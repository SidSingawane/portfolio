Fintech · Upstox

Simplifying Account Opening

The Upstox account opening flow was 24 screens. We got it to 16 without dropping a single compliance check. This is how.

Role
Product Designer
Timeline
4 Weeks
Team
Design, PM, Engineering, Compliance, Research
Skills
Product Design, UX Research, Interaction
Overview

Take the first thing every user does and make it shorter.

Account opening is the first thing every Upstox user does. If they can't get through it, nothing else matters. I audited every screen, read four months of drop-off data, and rebuilt the flow around two ideas: fewer steps, and one clear ask at a time.

Audit the field

Walked ten competitor flows end to end and audited every Upstox screen against them.

Read the data

Read four months of screen-by-screen drop-off data to find exactly where people left.

Rebuild around two ideas

Fewer steps, and asking one clear thing at a time. Everything else followed from those two.

Problem

Twenty-four steps, and the identity screens were a cliff.

Five things kept showing up in the data:

  • Twenty-four steps, with branches. Nobody knew how much longer they had to go.
  • Two big cliffs in the drop-off chart. PAN and DOB were losing 22% of starters. Personal Details lost another 18%.
  • We were asking for identity four times: PAN, then DOB, then Digilocker, then Aadhaar. Overlapping data, four ways to fail.
  • Micro-screens made decisions bigger than they needed to be. Picking a segment, a plan, a payment mode: each its own screen, each a chance to bail.
  • The screen at the end of the flow read like a receipt. No warmth, no product, no Upstox.
Opportunity

A quarter of starters never reach a first trade.

Roughly a quarter of the people who started a session never got to a first trade. That's the cost of the current flow, and it multiplies across millions of new accounts a year.

01
Recover the drop-off

About a quarter of starters never reach a first trade. Most of that leaks out at a handful of avoidable screens.

02
Small cuts, big multiples

Every step we don't need multiplies across millions of new accounts a year. Shaving the flow compounds fast.

03
First to feel calm and fast

Across the market, light flows were long and short flows felt heavy. Nobody had been both. That gap was ours to take.

Solution

Sixteen steps, one clear ask at a time.

The rebuilt flow keeps every mandated capture but folds the redundant asks together and reads as six plain chapters, in the order you actually walk through them.

Registration
Phone, OTP, email, segments
Personal Info
Auto-filled from Digilocker
Proof of ID & Address
Aadhaar · Digilocker once
Verify Bank
UPI first, manual fallback
Sign & Picture
Landscape sign, guided selfie
Nominee & E-sign
One Aadhaar OTP closes the loop
Core Flows

The six moves that carried the redesign.

The regulator's captures were fixed. These are the steps we'd added on top, reworked or removed.

01
Identity

One identity moment instead of three

The NSDL PAN check runs quietly in the background and users go straight into Digilocker. PAN and DOB no longer need their own screen. If the check fails, we ask inline. Three screens become one.

02
Removed

Kill the standalone plan screen

Pricing moves into the welcome video and inline help on the registration screen. T&C gets its own moment at consent, where it belongs. The screen goes without losing a word of disclosure.

03
Form ergonomics

A signature that fits, a selfie that works first try

Rotating the signature box to landscape gives real room to sign. The selfie step gets a live face mask and framing hints, so the photo passes the first time instead of the third.

04
Trust

UPI first, bank statement as backup

UPI app selection sits on the same screen as payment mode: one tap, one OTP, done. The slower account-aggregator path is there when UPI can't handle it. A short line above the field explains how the data is used, in plain English.

05
Compliance, gently

Nominee, moved up and explained in ten seconds

Nominee used to sit at the end and get skipped constantly. It moved earlier and got a ten-second explainer about the SEBI mandate. More people complete it, fewer skip it.

06
Welcome

An ending that feels like the beginning

The old closure screen was a receipt. The new one greets you by name, points you at the first thing to try, and puts a real face on it. It keeps feeling like Upstox at the finish line.

Research

Four months of drop-off data, three jobs every step had to do.

The data showed where people left. The synthesis showed what every screen had to pull off at once.

Zone 01 · 22% drop
Identity verification
What we saw

PAN and DOB was losing 22% of starters. People hesitated because they didn't remember their PAN, then Digilocker asked for the Aadhaar number and a six-digit PIN, both from memory. Second bounce, right there.

What was really going on

We were asking for identity three times when we only needed to check it once. Three chances to fail one thing.

Zone 02 · 18% drop
Personal details form
What we saw

Personal Details lost 18%. Too many fields at once. Trading experience, occupation, income: none of it obviously connected to opening an account.

What was really going on

We were asking for personal info before we'd earned it. People give it up later, once they can see where the flow is going. Not on screen four.

Zone 03 · Decision fatigue
Payment and plan
What we saw

A standalone plan screen, a separate payment-mode picker, a page of price and T&C. Three interruptions right when someone was almost done.

What was really going on

Three small screens doing the job of one. Each one added a fresh doubt at the point of least tolerance.

What every step had to do

Functional
Let me finish without getting lost

Show me where I am, how much is left, and what the next screen wants from me before it asks.

Emotional
Make it feel like the app I signed up for

From screen one I should feel like I'm inside Upstox, not a KYC form that Upstox happens to sit behind.

Behavioural
Give me a reason to trust each ask

Before I type my PAN, my bank details, or draw my signature, tell me why you need it and where it goes.

“The last screen felt impersonal and clinical.”

Exploring the Landscape

Every app nailed one axis and missed the other.

Ten broker flows walked end to end, on two axes: how heavy each screen feels, and how many screens the whole thing takes.

Light, but long
Groww
What they got right

Small priming screens tell you what's coming. A nice post-account experience. Auto-creates a UPI ID so people can skip the penny drop.

What they didn't

Light per screen, but the count adds up. By the end you've tapped a lot.

Light and short
Sahi PRO
What they got right

Genuinely delightful. Progress is visible at every step. Custom in-flow screens, like a UPI app picker, remove whole moments of friction.

What they didn't

Doesn't look like a trading app. And there's no lead mode while the account opens, so you're stuck watching a spinner.

Heavy but short
Dhan
What they got right

Fewer clicks. Multiple sub-steps live on one screen. No long priming.

What they didn't

Pages feel stuffed. PAN and Digilocker are asked for twice. Layouts nest and expand. There's no welcome once it's done.

“The apps that felt light were long. The apps that were short felt heavy. We wanted both.”

Prototyping & Testing

Two sixteen-step routes, tested against the old drop-off zones.

We carried two routes into moderated sessions with first-time investors and lapsed users, watching whether the identity collapse, the landscape signature, and the new ending actually held.

V0
Where we started

Twenty-four steps. Branched paths. Five bad drop-off zones. Visually heavy without being clear. This was the flow we were fixing.

V1
Explain first, ask second

Sixteen steps. Every screen opens with a small explainer: what we're about to ask, why, and where the data goes. Prevent the mistake before it happens.

V2
Ask first, explain if asked

Sixteen steps. Explainers hidden behind a tap. Confident users move fast; first-timers pull the context in when they need it.

V3
What we shipped

V1 on identity and bank steps, where the stakes are highest. V2 on personal details and nominee, where confident users want to move. Best of each, tested against the original drop-off zones.

Design Decisions

The two calls that shaped the build.

The two hardest, and why they went the way they did.

01
Identity

One identity check, not three

Decision

Run the PAN check in the background and send people straight to Digilocker, collapsing PAN, DOB, and Digilocker into one identity step.

Why

The drop-off data put the single biggest cliff, 22%, at the PAN and DOB screen. The root cause was repetition, not any one field: we were asking for identity three separate times. Checking once and reusing it removes two of the three chances to fail.

Rejected

Pre-filling everything from PAN alone. It couldn't satisfy the KRA address and photo requirements, so Digilocker still had to happen.

02
Disclosure

Explain first where it's risky, ask first where it's not

Decision

Lead with an explainer on identity and bank steps; hide the explainer behind a tap on personal details and nominee.

Why

In testing, explain-first prevented mistakes on the high-stakes screens, while confident users on the lighter screens just wanted to move. One rule for the whole flow helped one group and slowed the other.

Rejected

A single approach everywhere. Explain-first throughout dragged for confident users; ask-first throughout left first-timers exposed exactly where the stakes were highest.

Designing within Constraints

Real limits we designed inside.

01
Cut the flow without cutting compliance

SEBI, NSDL, KRA, and the depository each require specific fields, and those weren't going anywhere. The Digilocker and account-aggregator handshakes were third-party flows we couldn't restyle. The only steps we could cut were the ones we'd added on top: redundant identity asks, decision-fatigue mid-screens, and explainer pages that could just be inline text.

02
Heavy data, light room

This flow collects PAN, Aadhaar, bank data, a photo, a signature, nominee, and income info in one sitting. Any of those, on a bad screen, is a bailout. Every screen got one ask, one visual, and one clear signal of how much was left.

03
Make it feel like Upstox all the way through

For most of the flow, onboarding read like a form that Upstox happened to sit behind. The redesign treats it as part of the app instead: same voice, same type, same motion, right through to a closing screen that greets you by name.

A shorter flow, a lighter one, and an ending that fits.

Reflection

What this project changed my mind about.

Onboarding isn't the place to teach the product

Onboarding is where you help someone finish. Product tours land after the first trade, not before the first field. People sit through education once they've invested in something.

Identity checks shouldn't feel like a security queue

Asking for PAN, DOB, Aadhaar, and a PIN back to back is exhausting even when you have nothing to hide. Fetch it once, in the background, and surface a field only if the check fails.

Brand voice has to live in onboarding too

If the app feels warm and the onboarding reads like a form, people notice the seam. Rewriting the closure screen did more for retention than several flow-step cuts.

People sit through a long flow if they can see the end

Hide the progress bar and a short flow feels endless. Show it clearly and a long one becomes tolerable. Progress design is conversion design.
