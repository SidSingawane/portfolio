Transcript to Content Engine

One recorded conversation, six formats out the other side, still sounding like the person who said it. A tool for people who talk faster than they type.

Type
Self-initiated product
Role
Product Designer
Timeline
8 Weeks
Focus
Workflow design, AI editing UX

Overview

Every good podcast episode is a week of content if you can find the time to cut it up. Most people don't. So the ideas stay stuck in the audio. This is a workflow that takes one transcript and produces publication-ready pieces for every channel someone actually posts to.

The hard part wasn't the generation. The hard part was keeping every generated line traceable back to something the speaker actually said. Speed is easy. Speed without losing the person's voice is the whole game.

Problem

Turning a recording into content is slow, and the tools that speed it up tend to strip the personality out:

  • Transcripts land as a wall of text. No headings, no obvious way in.
  • Repurposing means bouncing between a text editor, a chatbot, and whatever tool the destination needs.
  • AI rewrites gravitate toward the same beige register no matter who was talking.
  • Nothing's connected. Fix a claim in the blog post and it's still wrong in the thread.

The Goal

One transcript goes in. Six channel-ready formats come out. The person still sounds like themselves, and every line can be traced to what they actually said.

Impact

Against how long the same person used to spend doing this by hand.

4x
Content pieces from the same one-hour recording
6

70%

Design Challenges

Making it fast without making it sound like everyone else.

01
Keeping the person's voice on the way out

Ask a generic AI to rewrite a transcript and it comes back sounding like every other AI-rewritten transcript. So I pulled voice signals from the transcript itself. Recurring phrases, sentence length, the words the speaker actually reaches for. Those become constraints on the rewrite. A punchy speaker stays punchy.

02
Reviewing six outputs without re-reading six times

If we generate six formats from one source, no one is going to proofread all six start to finish. I built a diff view that shows only what changed or got added relative to the transcript. You spot-check the deltas instead of reading everything.

Design Solutions

Three ideas that made the tool feel trustworthy instead of magical.

01
Feature

Every line traces back to the transcript

Hover any sentence in any generated piece and it shows you the exact span from the transcript it came from. One click plays that moment in the recording. If a claim looks off, you can check it in five seconds.

02
Feature

Formats are templates, not prompts

Blog post, thread, newsletter, script, pull quotes, summary. Each one has real structure and length rules baked in. You're not gambling on a prompt every time. What comes out looks the same shape whether it's the first transcript or the fiftieth.

03
Feature

A visible dial for how far you want to go from the source

One slider, from verbatim to polished. You can see where each output sits. Voice drift isn't something that quietly happens; it's a knob you turn.

Key Learnings

Three things I now believe about designing tools that generate text.

People trust AI content the moment they can trace it

The hover-to-source interaction did more for adoption than any model upgrade we shipped. Being able to check something turned out to matter more than being able to trust it blindly.

Treat each output type like a component, not a prompt

Once formats had real rules (length, structure, tone), we could reason about them the same way we reason about design system components. Six channels stopped being six moving targets.

The review step is where the design work actually is

Generation got fast years ago. Checking is still the slow part. Time spent designing the review surface paid back more than time spent tuning the model.

Reflection

Most of the design effort in an AI product should probably go into making the checking easy, not making the generation impressive. Generation is a solved problem for most tasks now. Verification is the thing that's still hard, and it's where the design lives.

The other realisation was that voice is data. Once we started treating it like something to extract and hold onto, rather than something to hope for on the output side, the whole pipeline got calmer.
