Fintech · Upstox

Homepage Reimagined

Rebuilding the Upstox home screen after it had quietly turned into a parking lot for every feature launch of the year. Three very different traders, one very crowded screen.

Role
Product Designer
Timeline
3 Weeks
Team
Design, PM, Engineering, Research
Skills
Product Design, Interaction, Visual Design
Overview

Rebuild the home screen as a system, not a pile.

The Upstox home screen is where every trader starts their day, and where every product team wants their newest widget. Over three weeks I re-ordered the same widgets around what each cohort actually opens the app to do.

Audit the pile

Scored nine Indian investing apps, and every widget on our own screen, for how easy the homepage was to read and move through.

Re-order, don't add

Gave each widget a role, then let the stack re-order around the person opening the app instead of bolting on anything new.

Ship on Android

Rolled out in stages with zero new widgets, to prove hierarchy alone could move the numbers.

Problem

Every launch bolted another widget onto the same screen.

Over about eighteen months the home screen piled up. Every widget earned its slot; together they were slowing people down instead of helping them find things. Five things kept coming up in the research:

  • Nobody could find anything. Even long-time users opened a widget just to check what it did.
  • Widgets looked alike. Momentum Gainers and Trending Options were, at a glance, the same card.
  • Time-sensitive moments were invisible. No cue when the market moved or a stock broke out.
  • The whole screen read like a list. No obvious starting point, no sense of what to look at first.
  • Nothing was grouped by why you'd use it. Discovery sat next to referrals sat next to portfolio summaries.
Opportunity

A crowded screen was about to get much more traffic.

Even in the mess, engagement was climbing fast. A broken screen was about to become a much bigger broken screen, so the time to fix the entry point was now.

01
Rising impressions

Impressions were up 3x period-over-period on Android. Far more people were landing on a screen with no hierarchy.

02
Doubling clicks

Clicks on the homepage had doubled, but nothing guided them. More attention, still no sense of where to look.

03
Climbing conversion

Order conversion was up 2.3x. Fixing the layout now would compound; waiting would quietly cap it.

Solution

One screen, re-ordered around who's opening it.

The same widgets, given roles and a real hierarchy, ordered by why each trader opens the app. Anchor first, then the things worth being shown, then the rest on demand.

Core Flows

Six moves, no new widgets.

Each one changed how the page reads without adding anything to it.

01
Anchor

Asset Grid as the anchor

A small grid of quick actions sits right at the top. Its shape never changes, but its contents shift with what you actually use. New launches like IPO and NCD get a small tag on the grid instead of a full banner across the screen.

02
Summary

Holdings as a summary, not a tab

The homepage version of Holdings is a small card. Today versus lifetime, one number, a link to the full screen. It's the daily check-in, not the deep audit. The proper screen still exists for people who want to sit with it.

03
Trust hero

Advisory looks the way it performs

Bigger type, a byline from the analyst, and enough space around it that your eye stops there. Nothing about it was scaled up. It just got the visual weight it had already earned.

04
Pattern

Every list gets its own look

Vertical, horizontal, and tabbed lists each got their own visual signature. Momentum Gainers and Trending Options stopped reading as the same widget. Different backgrounds, card weight, and icon shape tell them apart before you read the title.

05
Zone

All the promos in one predictable place

Referrals, offers, and UpLearn share one treatment and one spot near the bottom. Users learn where to find them without being interrupted by them. Banner blindness stops when banners stop being everywhere.

06
Recovery

Bring people back to the orders they forgot

Untriggered orders convert really well. When someone comes back after a day away, they see the ones still open with a timestamp and a soft nudge. A push notification helps the ones that aren't open yet.

Research

Three kinds of trader, three different questions.

The same screen, opened by three cohorts with very different things in their head.

Segment 01
Active FnO Traders
Who they are

Daily derivatives traders. Live inside the options smartlists, commodities, and their untriggered orders.

What they want

Speed. Two taps to a live instrument, three to a placed order. Don't make them scroll.

What's in their way

The Learn banners, the IPO promos, all the polite things between them and their tools.

Segment 02
Active Non-FnO Traders
Who they are

Regular equity investors. Hold portfolios, watch sectoral movers, read the odd advisory.

What they want

Ideas they can trust, something to compare against what they hold, a quick way to act on it.

What's in their way

Everything is derivatives-first. Their tools sit six scrolls down.

Segment 03
New Investors
Who they are

First six months on Upstox. Still learning what an SIP does, unsure what to look at first.

What they want

Somewhere to start, small trades to practise on, a screen that doesn't make them feel behind.

What's in their way

Everything looks the same and everything is a number. They need scaffolding before depth.

“Density is the easy answer. Hierarchy is the design problem.”

Audit note, week two

Exploring the Landscape

Everyone picked a side of calm versus busy; nobody won both.

Clean and airy
Groww
What they got right

Everything grouped into calm blocks. You always know where you are, and the screen breathes.

What they didn't

Cards look like reading, not clicking. No signal about what's hot right now, and the tab bar hogs the bottom.

Busy and full
Angel One
What they got right

Everything is one tap away, colour cues work, and if you know what you want you'll find it.

What they didn't

If you don't already know, you're lost. Promos keep interrupting, and nothing stands out because everything does.

Ambitious and unfocused
HDFC Sky
What they got right

A proper section for promotions so they don't leak everywhere, and research shows up early, which reads as trust.

What they didn't

Too many products fight for the same space, long paragraphs where a line would do, and it's not clear what's tappable.

What the audit told us

Density trains blindness

Apps that packed everything in taught users to ignore the promotional bits, then failed when a real promo dropped.

Airy hides depth

Beautiful, spacious apps buried the analytical depth serious traders needed to go deep.

Nobody served the whole ladder

No single home screen worked from first-week user all the way to daily options trader.

Prototyping & Testing

Three versions into research, then a fourth built from the wins.

Twelve traders, four from each cohort, moderated prototype walkthroughs one screen at a time.

V1
The gentle rewrite

Same widgets, same order. Only type weights and spacing changed. A cleaner quick-actions grid, a stronger Advisory tab, and the F&O and MTF buttons moved next to the indices they belong to.

V2.1
Same shape, more room

Kept the structure, loosened the spacing in the quick tiles, cleaned up the portfolio alignment. Different styles for the pill lists, plus new social proof on the Advisory cards.

V2.2
Quieter buttons

Softened the big primary CTAs like Activate F&O and Add Funds so they stopped competing with the content underneath. Colour, weight, and border all dialled down.

V3
What we shipped

V1's quick-access grid, V2.1's Advisory, V2.2's quieter buttons. Nothing new, just the parts of each version that had earned their keep.

Design Decisions

The two calls that shaped the build.

The hardest ones, and why they went the way they did.

01
Anchor

A tag on the grid, not another banner

Decision

Give launches a tag on the existing quick-actions grid instead of their own banner.

Why

Banners were the root of the banner-blindness problem, and each new one trained people to ignore the top of the page faster. A tag on a widget people already scan rides the attention that's there instead of fighting for new attention.

Rejected

A rotating banner carousel. It tested worst of all; people learned to scroll past the whole strip within a session.

02
Trust hero

Advisory earns weight, not size

Decision

Give Advisory heavier type, a visible byline, and room around it instead of making the card bigger.

Why

Advisory had the best click-to-order rate on the whole page, so it needed to draw the eye. Weight and space earn attention where raw size only adds noise.

Rejected

Scaling the card up. It would have made Advisory compete for real estate instead of quietly earning the look it deserved.

Designing within Constraints

Real limits the redesign had to live inside.

01
Everything wanted to be first

Every widget had a real reason to be there, and nobody would let us delete anything, so the fix had to be about ranking, not cutting. I sorted every widget into one of three buckets: anchor (used every session), hero (worth being shown to), or supporting (there when you need it). That ranking held across every version we drew.

02
One screen, three different traders

An FnO trader wants their options smartlist first; a new investor wants somewhere to start. Building three separate homepages was a bad idea, so I built one with a re-orderable stack that ships tuned to the person opening it. The layout stays familiar; the order moves.

03
No new widgets on the table

Leadership had to see that hierarchy alone could move the numbers before funding more real estate, and every promo team still needed a home for launches. So deleting widgets was never an option; re-ordering the ones we already had was the whole job.

Reflection

What I learned.

Density is a design problem, not a content problem

We didn't take out a single widget. We just changed how they related to each other. When everything on a screen has equal weight, nothing does.

Different traders want different defaults, not different apps

Personalising the order beats building three homepages. It got us most of the value for a fraction of the build.

Clicks lie; conversion tells you what to promote

Advisory had fewer clicks than the indices widget, but far more of them became orders. That ratio reshuffled the whole hierarchy.

Banner blindness ends when you stop spraying banners

Put every promo in one predictable zone and people learn where it is. They stop ignoring it, because it stops interrupting them.
