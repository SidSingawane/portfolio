Homepage Reimagined

Rebuilding the Upstox home screen after it had quietly turned into a parking lot for every feature launch of the year. Three very different traders, one very crowded screen.

Challenge

Every launch bolted another widget onto the home screen. Three very different trader types were all landing on the same overloaded page.

Move

Stop adding. Re-order the same widgets around what each cohort actually opens the app to do, and give the page a hierarchy instead of a pile.

Result

3× impressions and 2.3× orders period-over-period on Android, with zero new widgets added.

Industry
Fintech / Investing
Role
Product Designer II
Timeline
12 Weeks
Company
Upstox
Team

Overview

The Upstox home screen is where every trader starts their day. It's also where every product team at Upstox wants to place their newest widget. Over about eighteen months, the screen had piled up. Everything earned its slot; together they were slowing people down instead of helping them find things.

I rebuilt the homepage as a real system this time. Widgets have roles, the order changes depending on who's looking at it, and a new launch has somewhere obvious to go without breaking the whole layout.

Problem

Five things kept coming up in the research:

  • Nobody could find anything. Even long-time users would open a widget to check what it did.
  • Widgets looked alike. Momentum Gainers and Trending Options were, at a glance, the same card.
  • Time-sensitive moments were invisible. No cues when the market moved, when a stock broke out, when something was worth watching right now.
  • The whole screen felt like a list. No obvious starting point, no “here's what you should look at first”.
  • Nothing was grouped by why you'd use it. Discovery widgets sat next to referrals sat next to portfolio summaries.

Why This Problem Mattered

Even in the mess, engagement was climbing. Impressions were up 3x period-over-period on Android. Clicks doubled. Order conversion was up 2.3x. Which meant a broken screen was about to become a much bigger broken screen. Ship the redesign now, or watch the numbers tell us next quarter that the entry point can't keep up.

Industry Context

Everyone was piling on widgets. Nobody was fixing the layout underneath.

What we looked at

Nine Indian investing apps. Sahi, Angel One, Groww, Dhan, IndMoney, Share Market, ET Money, HDFC Sky, and our own. We scored each one on how easy the homepage was to read and how easy it was to move through, screenshot by screenshot, widget by widget.

What we saw

The trade-off kept repeating itself. Apps that packed everything in trained users to ignore the promotional bits, then failed when a real promo dropped. Apps that were beautiful and airy hid the analytical depth serious traders needed. Apps that got the beginner right often lost the intermediate. Nobody had one home screen that worked for the whole ladder from first-week user to daily options trader.

“Density is the easy answer. Hierarchy is the design problem.”

Audit note, week two

Research & Discovery

Three kinds of trader opening the same screen with three different questions in their head.

Segment 01
Active FnO Traders
Who they are

Daily derivatives traders. Live inside the options smartlists, commodities, and their untriggered orders folder.

What they want

Speed. Two taps to a live instrument, three taps to a placed order. Don't make them scroll.

What's in their way

The Learn banners. The IPO promos. All the polite things Upstox puts between them and their tools.

Segment 02
Active Non-FnO Traders
Who they are

Regular equity investors. Hold portfolios, watch sectoral movers, read the odd advisory.

What they want

Ideas they can trust. Something to compare against what they already hold. A quick way to act on it.

What's in their way

Everything is derivatives-first. Their tools sit six scrolls down.

Segment 03
New Investors
Who they are

First six months on Upstox. Still learning what a mutual fund SIP does. Not sure what to look at first.

What they want

Somewhere to start. Small trades they can practise on. A screen that doesn't make them feel behind.

What's in their way

Everything looks the same and everything is a number. They need scaffolding before they need depth.

Competitive Landscape

Three positions on the “calm vs busy” scale, all worth learning from.

Clean and airy
Groww
What they got right

Everything grouped into calm blocks. You always know where you are. The screen breathes.

What they didn't

Cards look like reading, not clicking. No motion, no signals about what's hot right now. The tab bar hogs the bottom of the screen.

Busy and full
Angel One
What they got right

Everything is one tap away. Colour cues work. If you know what you're looking for, you'll find it.

What they didn't

If you don't already know, you're lost. Promotional banners keep interrupting. Nothing stands out because everything does.

Ambitious and unfocused
HDFC Sky
What they got right

A proper section for promotions, so it doesn't leak everywhere. HDFC research shows up early, which counts as trust.

What they didn't

Too many products fighting for the same real estate. Long paragraphs where a line would do. It's not clear what's tappable.

“Nobody had figured out how to make finding things, doing things, and learning things live on the same calm screen.”

Audit summary

Problem Definition

Three jobs the homepage had to do for every trader, every time they opened the app.

Functional
Get me there fast

I know what I came here for. Get me to it in two taps and don't make me search.

Emotional
Tell me where to look

Don't make me read every card to figure out which matters. Show me what's important before I even scroll.

Behavioural
Catch me before the moment passes

A gainer, a breakout, an order I forgot to trigger. Surface it now, not in tomorrow's summary.

Design Challenges

Where the project kept getting hard.

01
Everything wanted to be first

Every widget on the screen had a real reason to be there. Nobody was going to let us delete anything. So the fix couldn't be about cutting; it had to be about ranking. I put every widget into one of three buckets: anchor (you use it every session), hero (you want to be shown to it), or supporting (it's there when you need it). That ranking held across every version we drew.

02
One screen, three different traders

An FnO trader opening the app wants their options smartlist first. A new investor wants somewhere to start. Same screen has to serve both. Building three different homepages was a bad idea, so I built one with a re-orderable stack that ships tuned to the person opening it. The layout stays familiar. The order moves.

03
Making Advisory stand out without shouting

Advisory had the best click-to-order rate on the whole page. The obvious move was to make it bigger. The obvious move was wrong. I gave it heavier type, room around it, and a visible byline from the analyst instead. It stopped competing for space and started earning attention.

Design Solutions

Six decisions that changed how the page felt.

Constraints we designed inside

No new widgets. Leadership had to see that hierarchy alone could move the numbers before anyone would fund more real estate. The re-order had to work for all three cohorts from a single layout (no per-cohort forks to maintain). And every promo team still needed a home for launches, so “just delete the banners” was never on the table.

01
Anchor

Asset Grid as the anchor

A small grid of quick actions sits right at the top. Its shape never changes, but its contents shift depending on what you actually use. New product launches (IPO, NCD) get a small tag on the grid instead of a full banner across the screen.

Decision

Give launches a tag on the existing grid instead of their own banner.

Why

Banners were the root of the banner-blindness problem. Each new one trained people to ignore the top of the page faster. A tag on a widget people already scan rides the attention that's there instead of fighting for new attention.

Rejected

A rotating banner carousel. It tested worst of all; people learned to scroll past the whole strip within a session.

02
Summary

Holdings as a summary, not a tab

The homepage version of Holdings is a small card. Today vs lifetime, one number, a link to the full screen. It's the daily check-in, not the deep audit. The proper screen still exists for people who want to sit with it.

03
Trust hero

Advisory looks the way it performs

Bigger type, a byline from the analyst, and enough space around it that your eye stops there. Nothing about it was scaled up. It just got the visual weight it had earned.

04
Pattern

Every list gets its own look

Vertical, horizontal, and tabbed lists each have their own visual signature. Momentum Gainers and Trending Options don't read as the same widget anymore. Different backgrounds, different card weight, different icon shape. You know which is which without reading the title.

05
Zone

All the promos, in one predictable place

Referrals, offers, UpLearn. Same visual treatment, same spot near the bottom. Users learn where to find them without being interrupted by them. Banner blindness stops when banners stop being everywhere.

06
Recovery

Bring people back to the orders they forgot

Untriggered Orders convert really well. When someone comes back after a day away, they see the ones still open with a timestamp and a soft nudge. A push notification helps the ones that aren't open yet.

Design Iterations

Three versions carried into research, each pushing further from the current design. The final one is what the versions taught us.

V1
The gentle rewrite

Same widgets, same order. Only the type weights and spacing changed. Quick actions got a cleaner grid, Advisory got a stronger tab treatment, and the F&O and MTF buttons moved next to the indices they belong to.

Why start here

A safe baseline. Long-time users would barely notice, so it told us how much of the problem was really about hierarchy vs layout.

V2.1
Same shape, more room

Kept the structure. Loosened the spacing in the quick tiles. Cleaned up the portfolio alignment. Different visual styles for the pill lists, plus a new social-proof treatment on the Advisory cards.

Why try it

To see how much hierarchy we could add without touching the layout. Turns out: quite a bit.

V2.2
Quieter buttons

Softened the big primary CTAs (Activate F&O, Add Funds) so they didn't compete with the content underneath. Colour, weight, and border all dialled down.

Why try it

Buttons had been shouting for months. Wanted to see what happened if they whispered instead.

V3
What we shipped

V1's quick-access grid. V2.1's Advisory. V2.2's quieter buttons. Nothing new; just the parts of each version that had earned their keep.

Why

Every version solved something. This one kept the wins and dropped the trade-offs.

Usability Testing

Twelve traders. Four from each cohort. Moderated prototype walkthroughs, one screen at a time.

What we wanted to find out

  • Does the re-ordered stack get active traders to their tools faster than the current design?
  • Does the new Advisory treatment make people trust it more, or does it just look nicer?
  • When we put three different list widgets in a row, can people tell them apart?
  • What's getting in the way when someone tries to pick up an untriggered order they left behind?

Key Learnings

Things I now believe about homepages, mostly because I learned them the hard way here.

Density is a design problem, not a content problem

We didn't take out a single widget. We just changed how they related to each other. When everything on a screen has equal weight, nothing does.

Different traders want different defaults, not different apps

Personalising the order beats making three homepages. It got us most of the value for a fraction of the build.

Clicks lie. Conversion tells you what to promote

Advisory had fewer clicks than the indices widget, but many more of those clicks became orders. That ratio reshuffled the whole hierarchy.

Banner blindness goes away when you stop spraying banners around

Put every promo in one predictable zone and people learn where it is. They stop ignoring it, because it stops interrupting them.

Future Opportunities

Where the homepage should go next.

RT
Read the session, not just the signup

Right now the cohort is fixed at signup. It should shift with the session. Derivatives-heavy on an expiry Thursday. Calmer on a Sunday afternoon.

DM
Show me what's happening right now

Sector spikes, breakouts, and unusual volume should come and go with the moment. They shouldn't live in a permanent widget that fades into the page.

XP
iOS and web, done properly

Android was first. iOS and web are next. Not a copy-paste port. Each platform gets what it's good at.

PUSH
The lock screen is the homepage too

A push notification should feel like the same conversation the app is having. Untriggered order pushes are the start; there's a lot more we could carry over.

SYS
Turn the widget roles into a real system

Anchor, hero, supporting. Those three labels became a shorthand internally. Formalise them as components and downstream teams get faster without asking design every time.
