Options Strategy Builder

A place inside Upstox where options traders can put a strategy together, see what it's risking, and place the order. All on one screen.

Challenge

Options traders analysed a strategy in Sensibull, then switched apps to place it. Two tools, one decision, a lot of tab-switching.

Move

Bring analysis and execution onto one screen: build the strategy, read the risk, place the order in place.

Result

Plan-and-place collapsed from two tabs to one; zero other apps needed to execute.

Overview

Upstox users would come to trade options and immediately open Sensibull in another tab to plan the actual trade. We watched it happen in session after session. Analysis on one screen, execution on another, and a lot of tab-switching in between.

This project brought the two together. Same app, same layout, one place to build a strategy, read the risk, and hit place order.

Problem

The old options flow had four things going against it:

  • People planned their trades on Sensibull. We only got the click at the end.
  • Every switch between tools broke concentration and lost context.
  • Greeks, payoff graphs, margin, and premium were spread across three different screens.
  • Users trusted us to place the order. They didn't trust us to help them decide what to place.

Why This Problem Mattered

Weekly expiries had pulled a whole new crowd into options trading. Volumes were up. Speculation was up. The tooling hadn't moved. Whoever solved the plan-to-execute gap first was going to own that trader for the rest of the year.

Impact

First quarter after launch, measured on the active F&O cohort. The builder shipped alongside an in-app education push, so it was a necessary part of these moves, not the sole cause.

Industry Context

Where the market was when we started.

The Rise of Retail Options Trading

By 2023 India's options market had gone vertical. Weekly expiries were the reason. A whole generation of retail traders had shown up on mobile, running multi-leg strategies on tools built for institutional desktops. It wasn't working, but the volume kept climbing.

The Complexity Challenge

Options are hard. You need to hold the Greeks in your head, read implied vol, weigh risk against reward across every leg, and time the entry right. Most Indian brokers went one of two ways: simplify it into something inaccurate, or dump the whole institutional layout on a 6-inch screen. Neither one helped a real trader make a real decision.

“Users trusted Upstox for execution, but not for decision-making.”

User Research Insight

Research & Discovery

We interviewed 18 traders across three groups. Same app, three very different jobs to do.

Segment 01
Beginner Traders
Who they are

First year in options. Learnt everything they know from YouTube. Small capital, weekly expiries, big appetite for content.

What they want

A safe way in. Ready-made strategies they can lift straight off the screen, with the downside spelled out before they hit buy.

What's stopping them

The Greeks make no sense. Multi-leg screens make less sense. They want help, but they don't want to feel like they need it.

Segment 02
Intermediate Traders
Who they are

Been at it for a year or two. Know their spreads from their straddles. Use Sensibull to plan and Upstox to place.

What they want

Fewer tab switches. Save their favourite structures and pull them up next Thursday.

What's stopping them

The two-tool workflow. Half the plan lives in Sensibull, half the trust lives in Upstox, and nothing connects.

Segment 03
Advanced Traders
Who they are

Daily traders. Know the Greeks the way most people know shortcuts. Time their entries to the second.

What they want

Live Greeks per leg. Keyboard-fast order entry. No help. Please, no help.

What's stopping them

The platform reads “consumer app”. They want the professional tool underneath the consumer skin.

Design Challenges

The hard bits.

01
Complexity vs Clarity

Options carry too many variables to show all at once and too much risk to hide any of them. We settled on progressive disclosure: what a beginner needs stays visible, what an advanced trader needs is one tap away, and nobody sees anything they don't.

02
Mobile Constraints

Most Upstox trading happens on a phone, and options had never really been designed for one. The payoff graph and the key metrics got the top of the screen. Per-leg Greeks, margin breakdown, and the fine print live one expand away.

03
Live Numbers, Steady Screen

Options prices don't sit still. Greeks tick, margin shifts, premium moves. The screen had to update every one of these without flashing at you. Small motion, coloured deltas for direction, and quiet numbers when nothing changed.

Design Solutions

Six things that took the builder from “fine” to “this is where I trade now”.

Constraints we designed inside

Order placement had to run through the existing F&O execution stack, with no new order types. Live option chains meant the screen re-rendered constantly, so nothing could depend on layout staying still. And it had to stay legible to first-time options traders without slowing down the pros who place ten trades a day.

01
Workspace

A Screen Just for Building

Full screen, one job. Traders add legs one at a time and the layout tightens as they go. What starts out with plenty of room turns into a working cockpit by leg four.

Decision

Give the builder its own full screen instead of a modal over the option chain.

Why

In testing, a modal kept traders anchored to the chain and they treated the builder as a lookup, not a workspace. A dedicated screen was the only version where people committed to constructing a multi-leg strategy.

Rejected

A bottom sheet. It capped at two legs before the payoff graph got squeezed off-screen.

02
Graph

The Payoff Graph Never Moves

Whatever you touch, the graph stays in the same spot on the screen and just redraws itself. Change a strike, watch the P&L curve shift. It's the fastest way to feel the trade before you place it.

03
Entry

Two Ways In, One Way Out

You can start from a blank canvas or pick a named strategy (Iron Condor, Bull Call Spread, Butterfly). Either route lands you in the same builder, so you don't have to relearn the app depending on your mood.

04
Layering

Depth on Demand

Greeks, margin breakup, per-leg breakeven. All there, none of it in your face. The main view stays about the decision. If you want the maths, you tap and get it.

05
Reuse

Save the Setup, Not the Numbers

Save a strategy today, open it next Thursday, and the strikes pick up whatever's live right now. The habit stays. The market catches up on its own.

06
Order

One Review Screen, Every Leg

Before you place the trade you see all of it: legs, margin, net premium, worst case. One tap places the whole basket. If part of it fills and part doesn't, the screen tells you what's live and what to do next.

Design Iterations

Five rounds. Each one shipped in prototype, went through testing, then went in the bin or into the next round.

V1
What broke

The first prototype tried to show Greeks, graph, legs, and the order form on one screen. Users tapped through it, blinked, and closed the app.

What we did

Broke the screen into zones with things you tap to see more. Overall density dropped by about 40%.

V2
What broke

Template picking was a separate screen. You'd go pick a strategy, come back, and forget what you were doing.

What we did

Pulled the templates into the builder as an overlay. You never actually leave the builder.

V3
What broke

The payoff graph was too small to read and you couldn't poke at it. Nobody used it.

What we did

Made it expandable to full width. Touch anywhere on the curve to see the P&L at that price. Suddenly people wouldn't place a trade without checking.

V4
What broke

Adding a leg on mobile took too many taps. Strike, type, quantity. Repeat. Users bailed.

What we did

Added a swipe-to-edit gesture and made the next leg default to the previous leg's settings. First-leg pain, but the second leg is quick.

V5
What broke

Confirmation screen felt cold. Users hesitated to hit the final button because they weren't sure what they were about to lose.

What we did

Added a card at the top showing worst case, best case, and where you break even — so the risk is felt before the order, not after.

Usability Testing

Twelve traders, moderated, one hour each. Four beginners, four intermediates, four who trade daily.

What we were checking

  • Does the layered-information idea work when someone new bumps into it?
  • Can we build a trade faster here than an intermediate trader can on Sensibull?
  • Is the payoff graph actually readable, or does it just look nice in the mock?
  • Where do people hesitate on the last screen?

Key Learnings

Things I'd have wanted to know a year earlier.

In fintech, the UI is the decision

You don't design a trading screen the way you design a settings page. Every pixel is either helping someone risk money more clearly, or getting in the way. Design for what happens after the tap, not the tap itself.

Show less, always

Showing everything makes people less confident, not more. Traders wanted control over what came into view and when. Layer the information. Let them pull.

Design the system, not the screen

One good screen is easy. Twelve screens that add up to a real workflow is the actual job. The payoff graph only works because the legs work, and the legs only work because the order screen closes the loop.

Ask the users, especially the experts

We were sure advanced traders wanted everything on one dense screen. They didn't. They wanted speed and confidence, in that order. If we hadn't sat with them we'd have shipped the wrong app.

Future Opportunities

Where I'd take the builder next if I picked it back up.

AI
Strategy nudges

Suggest a strategy that suits today's market, this user's history, and their risk tolerance. Show it, don't auto-apply it.

BT
Backtest before you buy

Try the strategy against last year's data. See how it would have done. Then decide with a real number in front of you.

SIM
What if the market moves

Sliders for spot price and volatility. Watch the payoff curve breathe. Useful for planning around events.

RR
Position sizing help

Based on the capital in the account and past trades, tell the user how much of this strategy they should probably take on.

SYNC
Pick up where you left off

Draft a strategy on your phone in the morning, come back to it on the desktop at lunch, place it wherever you are at expiry.
