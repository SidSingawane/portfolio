Options Strategy Builder

A place inside Upstox where options traders can put a strategy together, see what it's risking, and place the order. All on one screen.

Product Designer

Overview

Bring the whole options trade onto one screen.

Upstox traders were planning in one app and placing in another. Over seven weeks, my job was to collapse that into a single place to build a strategy, judge the risk, and execute.

Research

Sat with 18 traders across skill levels to find exactly where the flow broke.

Prototype & test

Built and tested five rounds, binning whatever didn't earn its place on the screen.

Ship one screen

Collapsed plan-and-place into a single builder that runs on the existing execution stack.

Problem

Analysis and execution lived in two different apps.

Upstox users came to trade options and immediately opened Sensibull in another tab to plan the trade: analysis in one app, execution in another, context lost in between. We watched it session after session.

  • People planned their trades on Sensibull. We only got the click at the end.
  • Every switch between tools broke concentration and lost context.
  • Greeks, payoff graphs, margin, and premium were spread across three screens.
  • Users trusted us to place the order, not to help them decide what to place.
Opportunity

Own the plan-to-execute gap.

Weekly expiries had pulled a new generation of retail traders onto mobile, and the tooling hadn't kept up. Whoever closed the gap between planning and placing first would own that trader.

01
Capture the whole trade

We only saw the final click. Bring the planning in and we own the full journey, not just the last step.

02
Earn decision trust

Traders trusted Upstox to execute, not to help them decide. Closing that gap deepens the relationship.

03
Ride the wave

A new crowd was entering options every week. First mover on plan-and-place keeps them here.

Solution

One screen to build, read the risk, and place the order.

The Strategy Builder lets a trader construct a multi-leg strategy, watch the payoff curve react, check the risk, and place the whole basket without ever opening a second app.

Core Flows

The flows that make it work.

Six moves cover almost everything a trader does in the builder.

01
Workspace

A screen just for building

Full screen, one job. Traders add legs one at a time and the layout tightens as they go. Plenty of room at the start, a working cockpit by leg four.

02
Graph

The payoff graph never moves

Anchored in one spot, full-width, and scrubbable along the curve. Change a strike, watch the P&L shift. It's the fastest way to feel the trade before you place it.

03
Entry

Blank canvas or named strategy

Start from scratch or pick an Iron Condor, Bull Call Spread, or Butterfly. Either route lands in the same builder, so nothing has to be relearned.

04
Layering

Depth on demand

Greeks, margin breakup, per-leg breakeven: all there, none of it in your face. Beginners aren't overwhelmed; pros tap once and get the maths.

05
Reuse

Save the setup, not the numbers

Save a strategy today, open it next Thursday, and the strikes pick up whatever's live right now. The habit stays; the market catches up on its own.

06
Order

One review screen, every leg

Before placing, you see all of it: legs, margin, net premium, worst case. One tap places the whole basket, and partial fills tell you what's live and what to do next.

Research

18 traders, three very different jobs.

Same app, three levels of trader, three different things they needed from it.

Segment 01
Beginner Traders
Who they are

First year in options, learnt it all on YouTube. Small capital, weekly expiries, big appetite for content.

The gap

They want ready-made strategies with the downside spelled out, but the Greeks and multi-leg screens make no sense.

Segment 02
Intermediate Traders
Who they are

A year or two in. Know their spreads from their straddles. Plan on Sensibull, place on Upstox.

The gap

They want fewer tab switches and saved structures for next Thursday, but nothing connects the two tools.

Segment 03
Advanced Traders
Who they are

Daily traders. Know the Greeks like keyboard shortcuts, time entries to the second.

The gap

They want live per-leg Greeks and keyboard-fast entry, but the platform reads “consumer app”.

Why one screen

Enhance the core app

Build inside the trading flow people already use, instead of asking them to leave for a tool.

Play to our strength

Upstox already owns execution. Attaching decision-making to it is the natural moat.

Cheaper to win

One well-designed screen beats a separate analytics product on cost and adoption.

Prototyping & Testing

Five rounds, each tested and cut or kept.

We put each round in front of 12 traders, four at each skill level, and watched where they hesitated.

V1
Too much at once

Greeks, graph, legs, and the order form all on one screen. Users tapped through it and left. We broke it into layers and cut density by about 40%.

V3
The graph earned its place

The payoff graph was too small to read and static, so nobody used it. Made it full-width and touchable; after that, people wouldn't trade without checking it.

V5
Risk, felt before the order

The confirmation screen felt cold and people hesitated. A card showing worst case, best case, and breakeven moved the risk to before the tap.

“I always want the graph visible. It's the one thing that tells me if my strategy makes sense.”

Intermediate Trader, 2yr exp.

“Too many numbers at once. I just need to know: what's my max loss and when do I break even?”

Beginner Trader, 4mo exp.

“I'd use this weekly if I could save strategies. Right now I rebuild the same Iron Condor every Thursday.”

Advanced Trader, 5yr exp.
Design Decisions

The calls that shaped the build.

The two hardest, and why they went the way they did.

01
Workspace

A dedicated screen, not a modal

Decision

Give the builder its own full screen instead of a modal over the option chain.

Why

In testing, a modal kept traders anchored to the chain and they treated the builder as a lookup, not a workspace. A dedicated screen was the only version where people committed to constructing a multi-leg strategy.

Rejected

A bottom sheet. It capped at two legs before the payoff graph got squeezed off-screen.

02
Layering

Progressive disclosure over one dense screen

Decision

Keep the decision-critical view visible and put Greeks and margin one tap away.

Why

The first prototype showed everything at once and users bounced. Layering cut density by about 40% and matched how people actually read a trade.

Rejected

A single dense “pro” screen. Even advanced traders wanted speed and confidence over everything-at-once.

Designing within Constraints

Real limits we designed inside.

01
Complexity vs Clarity

Options carry too many variables to show all at once and too much risk to hide any of them. Progressive disclosure kept what a beginner needs visible and an advanced trader's detail one tap away.

02
Mobile Constraints

Most Upstox trading happens on a phone, and options had never really been designed for one. The payoff graph and key metrics got the top of the screen; the fine print lives one expand away.

03
Live Numbers, Steady Screen

Greeks tick, margin shifts, premium moves. The screen had to update all of it without flashing. Small motion, coloured deltas for direction, and quiet numbers when nothing changed.

Reflection

What I learned.

In fintech, the UI is the decision

Every pixel is either helping someone risk money more clearly or getting in the way. Design for what happens after the tap, not the tap itself.

Show less, always

Showing everything made people less confident, not more. Traders wanted control over what came into view and when. Layer the information; let them pull.

Ask the experts, especially

We were sure advanced traders wanted everything on one dense screen. They didn't. Sitting with them saved us from shipping the wrong app.
